is a dispute with four clauses in play, and the constraint that makes it a game is consistency: an advocate who argues literalism on clause one and commercial purpose on clause three will be held to the inconsistency by Claude, whose policy is to quote the player's own submissions back at them. So the player must choose an interpretive posture for the case as a whole and then find the best result available within it, which is exactly the discipline appellate advocates operate under. Three further mechanics deepen this. First, contra proferentem is available only against the proposer, and the player must establish who proposed each clause, which is an evidential move with a cost, and which may reveal that their own client proposed two of the four. Second, extrinsic material (prior drafts, negotiation correspondence) is available at a cost and cuts both ways, so a player who opens the negotiation record to support a contextual reading may hand the counterparty a better reading of a different clause. Third, the boundary with implication is policed: where the player's contextual reading is really an attempt to add a term, the court will say so and the argument fails, which forces precision about whether the words used can bear the meaning proposed. Round three delivers the counterparty's opening submission, and the player must decide whether to hold their posture or switch, knowing that switching costs credibility and that holding may cost the point. Failure mode is a player who wins three clauses on a literal approach and loses the fourth, which happens to be the valuable one, to the same approach.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 9 and 29 with CPA s 2(46), and interpretive principles developed through case law: the words are read in their documentary and commercial context, business common sense informs the choice between rival readings where the language is genuinely ambiguous, ambiguity in a term may be construed against the party who proposed it under the contra proferentem principle, and interpretation is distinct from implication because it assigns meaning to language actually used.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that interpretation is a system rather than a set of tools to be picked individually, and that the strategic unit is the case rather than the clause. This is the meta or interpretive axis in its purest form.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by achieving the best aggregate value across all four clauses within one consistent posture. Lose instructively by winning individual clauses through incompatible arguments and being held to the contradiction.
