runs eight quarters from the debtor's chair. The player has limited cash each quarter and six debts, and must decide both how much to pay and how to appropriate it. The physics is that intimation at the time of payment is everything: an express intimation under s 59 binds the creditor, silence hands the option to the creditor under s 60, and if the creditor also stays silent the s 61 default applies payments in order of time, which may be the worst outcome of all where the oldest debt is unsecured and low-interest. The strategic problems compound. The player generally wants to protect secured debts and preserve the running of limitation on debts they hope will lapse, while the creditor (Claude, with a policy of appropriating to the debt closest to being time-barred and then to the least secured) wants the opposite. Quarter three introduces a payment made by a third party on the debtor's behalf, and the player must work out whose intimation counts. Quarter five introduces a guarantor whose exposure depends on which debt was discharged, so appropriation now affects a party outside the ledger, and the player's cheapest appropriation may destroy a relationship they need. Quarter seven presents a partial payment made under pressure with no time to compose an intimation, and the player must decide whether to send a written appropriation afterwards, which the game treats as ineffective because s 59 requires intimation at the time of payment or circumstances implying it, which is a strict rule and an instructive loss. Failure mode is a debtor who pays consistently and silently for two years and discovers that every rupee went to the debts they most wanted to lapse.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 59 to 61. Section 59 gives the debtor, where he owes several distinct debts and makes a payment with express intimation or under circumstances implying that it is to be applied to a particular debt, the right to have it so applied. Section 60 provides that where the debtor omits to intimate and there are no circumstances indicating to which debt the payment applies, the creditor may apply it at his discretion to any lawful debt actually due, including one whose recovery is barred by the law of limitation. Section 61 provides that where neither party makes an appropriation, the payment is applied in discharge of the debts in order of time, and if they are of equal standing, proportionately.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that a payment is a legal act carrying an election, and that the election expires at the moment of payment. This is one of the clearest instances in the Act of a right that exists only if exercised contemporaneously, and it pairs with the notice rule in Module 55.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by ending eight quarters with the secured debts discharged and the intended debts time-barred. Lose instructively by paying without intimation, which quietly transfers the option to the creditor.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay as the creditor exercising s 60, which reveals the full value of the option the debtor was giving away.
