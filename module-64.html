Enter The Remedy Hand

SYSTEM MODEL

A card hand of remedies (rectification, rescission, cancellation, perpetual injunction, mandatory injunction, damages in lieu, compensation, restoration). Each card has prerequisites and incompatibilities. Readable state: for each card, whether its prerequisites are met on the current facts, and a Coherence indicator showing whether the current combination is internally consistent.

is a full pleading and relief exercise across a dispute with several strands: an instrument that misstates the agreed boundary, a contract induced by misrepresentation, a negative covenant the defendant is breaching, and benefits that have passed both ways. The player must select and sequence relief, and the game enforces coherence strictly, because inconsistent relief is the most common failure in real pleadings and the most invisible in teaching materials. The decisions build on each other. Rectification and rescission are mutually exclusive on the same instrument, so the player must decide whether their client's story is that the document is wrong (rectify and then enforce) or that the transaction should never have happened (rescind and restore). Injunctive relief under ss 36 to 42 requires the player to consider whether damages would be adequate, and s 40's power to award damages in lieu means the player may win the argument and receive money rather than the injunction, which is a real risk where the client wants the behaviour stopped. Restoration under ss 30 and 33 and under ICA ss 64 and 65 attaches to rescission and cancellation, so the player must compute what their own client will have to give back, which mirrors the lesson from Modules 36 and 58. Round three introduces the client's actual priority, discoverable only by asking, and it turns out to be stopping the defendant's conduct rather than unwinding the transaction, which makes the injunction the centre of the case and demotes everything else. Round four is the hearing, where Claude, as the bench, tests the coherence of the combination and refuses relief that contradicts other relief sought. Failure mode is a player who pleads comprehensively, is met with the observation that they cannot both affirm and disaffirm, and is put to an election at the worst moment.

Resolver notes

Encode remedies as objects with prerequisites and an incompatibility matrix. Coherence is checked across the selected set and must name the specific contradiction. Section 40 damages in lieu should fire probabilistically where damages would be adequate, so the injunction is never a certainty. Client priority is discoverable only through an interview action.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

SRA ss 26 to 33 and 36 to 42, with ICA ss 64 to 66 and 75. Section 26 permits rectification of an instrument that, through fraud or mutual mistake, does not express the real intention of the parties. Sections 27 to 30 govern rescission of contracts and the court's power to require the party to whom relief is granted to restore benefits and make compensation. Sections 31 to 33 govern cancellation of instruments and restoration of benefits. Sections 36 to 42 govern preventive relief by injunction, temporary and perpetual, mandatory injunctions, damages in lieu of or in addition to injunction, and the circumstances in which an injunction is refused, including where the plaintiff seeks to enforce a negative agreement.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that remedies carry implicit theories of the transaction, and that pleading is therefore an exercise in choosing a story. This closes World 9 by tying the remedial architecture back to the characterisation work in World 7.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by obtaining a coherent package that delivers the client's actual priority. Lose instructively by pleading incompatible relief, or by winning rescission for a client who needed an injunction.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay with the client's priority changed to unwinding the transaction, which makes an entirely different combination correct on identical facts.
