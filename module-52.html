SYSTEM MODEL

is played from the promisor's chair, which is where the strategy lives. The player is one of four joint promisors on a failed project, and is the most solvent, which means the promisee will come for them first. Their objective is to minimise net exposure across three rounds, and the tools are all found in ss 42 to 45. The decisions are genuinely difficult. Should the player perform early and voluntarily, which caps the loss and preserves the relationship but starts the contribution problem from the largest possible base? Should they negotiate a release from the promisee, which under s 44 does not discharge the others and may therefore be obtainable more cheaply than the player expects, since the promisee loses less by releasing one than intuition suggests? Should they seek an express agreement varying s 43's default before the claim crystallises, which is cheap now and impossible later? Round two introduces the insolvency of one promisor, and s 43's rule that the default is shared equally among the remaining promisors converts a third party's failure into the player's cost, which is the moment most players understand joint liability properly. Round three tests personal performance under s 40: one obligation was of a kind the promisee was entitled to have performed by a specific promisor, so substituting another does not discharge it, and the player must identify which obligation that was. Failure mode is a player who settles generously with the promisee and then finds the contribution claims against their co-promisors worthless.

Opponent policy

The opposing side attacks the weakest proved element, then adapts to any repeated tactic.

Resolver notes

Model joint promisors as a set with solvency, faultShare and releaseState. Section 43 recovery must permit full recovery from any one; contribution must redistribute per the statutory default including the equal-sharing rule on default. Section 44 must expressly not discharge the others. Section 40 personal performance is a flag on specific obligations.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 37 and 40 to 45. Section 37 requires parties to perform or offer to perform their promises unless dispensed with or excused. Section 40 requires personal performance where it appears the parties intended the promise to be performed by the promisor personally. Section 42 requires joint promisors, during their joint lives, to fulfil the promise jointly. Section 43 allows the promisee, in the absence of express agreement, to compel any one or more of the joint promisors to perform the whole, and gives the performing promisor a right of contribution, with a joint promisor's default shared equally among the rest. Section 44 provides that a release of one joint promisor does not discharge the others. Section 45 vests the right to claim performance in joint promisees jointly.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that joint liability allocates litigation risk rather than substantive responsibility, and that the real contest is over contribution rather than over the promisee's claim, which is where the money is actually decided.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by ending with net exposure below the player's proportionate fault share. Lose instructively by paying the whole and recovering nothing in contribution, or by relying on a co-promisor to discharge an obligation that required personal performance.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay as the promisee choosing whom to sue, where the optimal strategy is not simply to sue the richest but to sue the one whose contribution claims will be hardest to run.
