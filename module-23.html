SYSTEM MODEL

A distressed debt book with five debtors of varying solvency. Readable state per debtor: outstanding amount, realistic recovery value, limitation clock, security held, and a Concession Log recording anything the creditor has said or done that might amount to remission or extension under s 63.

runs a workout across four quarters. The player is the creditor with a recovery target, five debtors, and limited enforcement capacity so they cannot pursue everyone. The engine is that every concession is legally effective at once. An extension of time under s 63 is binding, so it cannot be withdrawn when the market turns; a remission of part is a discharge of that part, so it cannot be revived when the debtor's fortunes improve, which happens to one debtor in quarter three and is the game's cruellest and most instructive moment. Three strategic problems run in parallel. First, sequencing: conceding to the weakest debtor first is humane and sets a precedent the other four will invoke, and Claude, playing the debtors collectively, does invoke it. Second, the limitation clock, which interacts with concessions in ways the player must think about, because an extension granted casually can leave the creditor with a debt that has become difficult to enforce, and the s 25(3) route in Module 21 then becomes the only repair. Third, the boundary with s 62: accepting a substituted obligation from a third party or on materially different terms may be a novation that discharges the original contract entirely, including securities, rather than a remission of part, and the player must decide in quarter two whether to accept a restructured obligation from a debtor's group company, which improves the covenant and may silently release the security. Failure mode is a creditor who extends generously in quarter one, discovers in quarter three that the improved debtor cannot be pursued for the remitted portion, and has no security left on the restructured one.

Resolver notes

Every player utterance to a debtor must be classified by a resolver into remission, extension, satisfaction, novation or no concession, and logged irreversibly. Novation must trigger a security-release check. Limitation is a per-debt clock affected by extensions and acknowledgments.

The controlling legal fact was identified.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA s 63, under which a promisee may dispense with or remit wholly or in part the performance of the promise made to him, extend the time for performance, or accept any satisfaction he thinks fit instead of performance. Section 63 requires no fresh consideration, which distinguishes Indian law from the English rule in Foakes v Beer. Section 62 (novation, rescission and alteration) and s 25 mark the boundaries of the provision.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that Indian law makes creditor concessions cheap to give and impossible to take back, which inverts the strategic advice a common-law-trained instinct would produce. This also sets up Module 57 on novation and Module 62 on damages clauses, since both concern what a party gives up by agreement rather than by breach.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by hitting the recovery target across four quarters with securities intact. Lose instructively by remitting portions of debts that later became fully recoverable, or by converting a remission into an unintended novation.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay under the English rule (part payment not discharging the balance without fresh consideration), togglable in the game, so the player can see how much of the strategy is created by s 63 alone.
