SYSTEM MODEL

A day in a city with six transactions where terms travel alongside a benefit: boarding a bus, sitting down at a restaurant, using a free trial, parking in a private lot, accepting a delivered sample, and continuing to use a service after a notified change. Readable state per transaction: what the player has physically done, what terms were attached, and an Acceptance Ledger showing which promises the player has now made without saying a word.

MODEL VAR // Benefit TimingMODEL VAR // Terms TimingMODEL VAR // Affirmative Act RequiredMODEL VAR // Legal Position

sets a goal that sounds impossible and is not: obtain the benefit of all six services while accepting the terms of only two. This forces the player to look at each transaction and find where the conduct-equals-acceptance link is weakest. Some links are unbreakable, because taking the ride simply is acceptance under s 8; those transactions must be paid for or skipped, and part of the learning is recognising which ones they are. Others are breakable, and the ways of breaking them are precise legal moves: rejecting the sample rather than consuming it, terminating the trial before the notified change takes effect, parking in a lot where the notice appeared after the barrier rather than before it. The second half introduces the mirror problem. The player becomes a service designer whose revenue depends on conduct amounting to acceptance, and Claude plays a sophisticated consumer running exactly the moves the player just used. The designer must decide where to place the terms, when to place them relative to the benefit, and whether to require an affirmative act (higher legal certainty, lower conversion) or to rely on continued use (higher conversion, weaker acceptance). This is where the game generates its 'aha': the player discovers that the doctrinal question of when conduct accepts is identical to the design question of where in a flow to put the terms, which sets up Modules 48 and 51 directly. Failure mode is the designer who relies on continued use for a materially onerous term and finds that Claude's consumer took the benefit and repudiated the term.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 8 and 9. Section 8 makes performance of the conditions of a proposal an acceptance. Section 9 provides that a promise is express where the proposal or acceptance is made in words, and implied where it is made otherwise than in words, so conduct can constitute both proposal and acceptance.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player sees that s 9 makes silence-adjacent behaviour legally productive, and that almost all consumer contracting in India runs on this provision rather than on signatures.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

REPLAY HOOK

Replay the consumer half with an added constraint that the player must also preserve a consumer complaint under the CPA, which forbids some of the cheapest avoidance moves.
