SYSTEM MODEL

A small lending and trading business assessing five counterparties, two of whom are minors and one of whom is close to majority. Readable state per counterparty: apparent age, documentary evidence of age, the nature of the transaction, security taken, and a Recoverability indicator that reads zero for a minor regardless of every other attribute on the screen.

is a full underwriting cycle with a portfolio target and a verification budget. Verification is costly and slows deals, and competitors will take any business the player refuses, so refusing to lend to anyone who looks young is not a viable strategy. The player must therefore allocate verification spend by risk, and the game rewards reading the transaction rather than the person: a small sale of goods to a young buyer is a different exposure from a mortgage. Three mechanics create the depth. First, the player can take security, guarantees or a parent's co-signature, and the game distinguishes sharply between these: security given by the minor is worthless because the underlying agreement is void, while an independent obligation from a competent adult is not, and learning that distinction is the module's commercial core. Second, the misrepresentation route is closed, and Claude, playing the minor's guardian, will plead minority even where the minor lied, so the player who relies on the declaration loses. Third, a partial escape exists but only for necessaries under s 68, which points forward to Module 27 and applies to the minor's property rather than to the minor personally, so it works for some transactions in the book and not others. Round four brings a portfolio review where every void transaction is written off, and the player sees which underwriting decisions actually protected them.

The controlling legal fact was identified.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 10 and 11 with s 3 of the Indian Majority Act, 1875. Section 11 makes competence to contract depend on majority, sound mind and not being disqualified by law. The consequence, that an agreement by a minor is void from the beginning, comes from case law rather than express statutory words: in Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose (1903) 30 IA 114, the Privy Council held a minor's mortgage void ab initio, rejected estoppel where the lender's agent knew of the minority, and held that ss 64 and 65 did not apply because there was no contract between competent parties.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns why voidness is a harsher status than voidability from the counterparty's point of view, and learns that the only real protections are verification before contracting and an independent obligation from a competent person, both of which are pre-transaction moves.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by hitting the portfolio target with write-offs below threshold. Lose instructively by relying on a declaration of age, on security given by the minor, or on the assumption that a lie shifts the risk.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay with the verification budget halved, which forces the player to protect by structure (adult co-obligors) rather than by information.
