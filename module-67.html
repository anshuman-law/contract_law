SYSTEM MODEL

A completed transaction with benefits that have moved in both directions: money paid, property transferred, services consumed, goods resold, and improvements made. Readable state: a restitution ledger valuing each item at receipt and at present, whether the benefit survives in specie or must be compensated for, and a Net Position figure showing what the player's client actually walks away with if the transaction is unwound.

runs three parallel unwindings with different legal characters, which forces the player to track the doctrinal routes rather than treating restitution as one rule. The first is a voidable contract the client wishes to rescind under s 19, where s 64 requires the rescinding party to restore benefits received. The second is an agreement discovered to be void, where s 65 obliges any person who received an advantage to restore it, so the obligation runs both ways and does not depend on who wants out. The third is a contract that becomes void under s 56, where s 65 applies to advantages received before frustration and the timing of receipt matters. The strategy sits in preparation and sequencing. The player has four preparatory moves available before initiating any unwinding: valuing benefits at receipt (which fixes the measure favourably if values have since risen), documenting consumption (which converts an in-specie restoration into a compensation claim the player may prefer), segregating what has been received so it survives in identifiable form, and dealing with a third party who acquired part of the property. Each move costs time, and the counterparty's position deteriorates during the delay, so waiting is not free. Round three introduces SRA ss 30 and 33, where the court may impose restoration as a condition of granting relief, which means the player may be ordered to restore more than s 64 alone would require, and the player must decide whether to seek court-ordered rescission (cleaner, conditional) or to rescind by notice and litigate afterwards. Failure mode is a client who rescinds enthusiastically, restores everything at present values, and would have been better off performing.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 64 and 65, with SRA ss 30 and 33. Section 64 provides that when a person at whose option a contract is voidable rescinds it, the other party need not perform, and the rescinding party must, if he has received any benefit from another party to the contract, restore that benefit so far as may be. Section 65 provides that when an agreement is discovered to be void, or when a contract becomes void, any person who has received any advantage under it is bound to restore it or make compensation for it to the person from whom he received it. SRA s 30 empowers the court, on adjudging rescission, to require the party to whom relief is granted to restore benefits and make compensation, and s 33 does the same on cancellation of an instrument.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that avoidance and restitution are a single machine, and learns the practical discipline of running the restitution ledger before advising a client to rescind. This retrospectively reframes Modules 4, 29, 36 and 58, all of which produced a right to escape without pricing it.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by achieving the best Net Position across the three unwindings. Lose instructively by unwinding a transaction whose restitution cost exceeds the benefit of escape, which is the commonest real-world error in rescission advice.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay where the client has consumed nothing and improved nothing, which makes unwinding cheap and reveals how much the earlier difficulty came from the client's own dealings with the benefit.
