puts the player in the supplier's chair with a working-capital clock. The department is in a hurry, an oral assurance has been given, and the formally executed agreement is stuck in approvals. Every week of delay costs the supplier money, and the department official (Claude) applies steady pressure with a policy of promising regularisation later and escalating urgency, never refusing outright. The physics is unforgiving: performance without a compliant art 299 instrument produces no enforceable contract, and the supplier's only route is s 70, which requires that the act was lawful, non-gratuitous, and that the Government enjoyed the benefit. The decisions are all about sequencing under cost. Should the supplier halt and lose the working-capital clock, or perform and build a restitution file? Building that file well is itself a set of moves: obtaining written acknowledgment of receipt, ensuring delivery is to the department rather than to an individual, avoiding any language suggesting the work was done gratuitously or as a favour, and keeping the claim framed as compensation for benefit taken rather than as damages for breach, because the two are legally distinct and pleading the wrong one wastes the claim. Round three introduces the officer's transfer, which removes the friendly interlocutor and tests whether the file the player built stands on its own. The failure mode that teaches most is the supplier who performs on an oral assurance, pleads breach of contract, and loses on art 299 without having pleaded s 70 in the alternative.

Why the system resolved this way

CORE RUN COMPLETE

The controlling legal fact was identified.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

Constitution arts 298 and 299, with ICA s 10 and s 70. Article 299(1) requires that contracts made in exercise of the executive power of the Union or a State be expressed to be made by the President or the Governor and be executed on their behalf by a duly authorised person. The Supreme Court in Chatturbhuj Vithaldas Jasani v Moreshwar Parashram, AIR 1954 SC 236, held on the facts there that non-compliant contracts were not void for all purposes, but that reasoning was substantially course-corrected by later decisions (including the Mulamchand and Rallia Ram line), and the prevailing position is that non-compliance renders the contract unenforceable against the Government, with the supplier's remedy lying in restitution under ICA s 70 where the Government has taken the benefit. The tension between these lines is itself the teaching point and should be presented as unresolved rather than flattened.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that constitutional form allocates risk rather than merely regulating paperwork, and that the practical skill is running a contractual and a restitutionary theory in parallel from day one because you cannot retrofit the second after the first fails.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by recovering value for what was supplied while remaining solvent. Lose instructively by holding an unenforceable contract with a well-documented breach claim and no restitutionary pleading, which is the most common real-world failure in government contracting.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay from the department's side, where the objective is to obtain urgent supply without exposing the exchequer, which surfaces why art 299 exists at all.
