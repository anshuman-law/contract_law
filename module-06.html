SYSTEM MODEL

A retail and procurement environment where the player designs five channels: a shop window display, a printed catalogue, an online listing, a public auction, and a tender notice. Each channel has drafting parameters (language of commitment, stated stock, reservation of rights, whether a price is described as fixed). Readable state: for each channel, who currently holds the trigger, plus an Exposure meter counting how many contracts could be forced on the player simultaneously.

is a design exercise with a mischief. The player runs a seasonal sale and wants maximum customer commitment with minimum seller commitment, which is the actual commercial goal behind almost every retail communication. They draft each of the five channels, and Claude then plays a hostile market: a bulk buyer who tries to accept the listing at the advertised price for the entire stock, a bargain hunter who tenders exact cash at the window display, and a tender participant who claims the notice was itself a proposal. Each attack targets a different drafting weakness. The trade-off that makes this a game rather than a classification drill is that every protective drafting move costs conversion. Adding 'subject to availability and confirmation' keeps the trigger with the seller but reduces customer trust and, in the auction channel, reduces bids. Stating a fixed price with no reservation maximises conversion but converts the listing into something a court may read as a proposal, especially where stock is stated and the language is committal. The auction channel adds a second layer, because the auctioneer's call for bids is an invitation, each bid a proposal, and the fall of the hammer the acceptance, so the player must decide whether to reserve the right to withdraw and thereby depress bidding. Round three introduces a pricing error, which is the classic stress test: the player discovers a listing at one tenth of cost with two hundred pending customer actions, and must determine, channel by channel, how many of those actions were acceptances they are now bound by.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 2(a) and 2(b). A proposal exists where one person signifies to another a willingness to do or abstain from doing anything with a view to obtaining that other's assent. Advertisements, catalogues, price displays and tender notices are ordinarily construed as invitations to offer rather than proposals, so the customer or bidder makes the proposal and the trader or authority accepts or refuses.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player stops treating the offer and invitation distinction as taxonomy and starts seeing it as an allocation of the option to refuse, which is exactly what it is worth commercially. This is the interpretive axis operating in the drafting room rather than the courtroom.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

REPLAY HOOK

Replay as the bulk buyer hunting for the one channel that was drafted as a proposal, which is the mirror skill and the one litigators actually use.
