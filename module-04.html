SYSTEM MODEL

A live contract with a status bar that can read Valid, Voidable at A's option, Voidable at B's option, or Void, plus a countdown showing how long the current status has persisted. Readable state: accrued benefits on each side, the market price of the subject matter (moving), and an Affirmation counter that ticks up every time the player performs after learning of the defect.

gives the player the option-holder's seat in a machinery supply contract, having just discovered a misrepresentation that makes the contract voidable at their option under s 19. The market price of the machinery is moving, and here is the engine of the game: avoidance is worth a lot when the market has fallen and nothing when it has risen, so the player is tempted to wait and see. But every act of performance after knowledge feeds the Affirmation counter, and once affirmation is complete the option is gone permanently. Claude plays the counterparty with a policy of pushing performance milestones at the player precisely to burn the option. Three further pressures make this rich. First, a s 56 event (a temporary import restriction) is dangled in round two, and the player must judge whether it merely makes performance harder or strikes at the root, because a genuine s 56 event makes the contract void for both sides and destroys the player's leverage entirely. Second, if the player avoids, s 64 requires them to restore benefits received, so avoidance is expensive and the player must compute whether they can afford it. Third, a third party acquires an interest in the goods midway, which in real transactions is what usually forecloses rescission. The failure mode is the player who holds the option too long waiting for a better price and discovers in round four that they have affirmed by conduct.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 2(g), 2(h), 2(i), 2(j), 19, 19A and 56. A void agreement is not enforceable by law; a voidable contract is enforceable at the option of one party and remains fully binding until avoided; s 19 makes a contract caused by coercion, fraud or misrepresentation voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so caused; s 19A does the same for undue influence and allows the court to set aside on terms; s 56 makes a contract void when performance becomes impossible or unlawful.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

Voidability is revealed as a depreciating asset rather than a permanent shield. That reframing is the whole practical difference between void and voidable, and it explains why counsel advising on fraud asks first how long the client has known.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by exiting or affirming at the point of maximum value while the option is still legally alive. Lose instructively by losing the option through unwitting affirmation, or by avoiding at a moment when restoration under s 64 costs more than the contract was worth.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay as the wrongdoer, whose entire strategy is to keep the counterparty performing until affirmation is complete, which teaches the defensive value of prompt, documented milestones.
