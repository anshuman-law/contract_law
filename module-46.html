is a claim-building exercise in which the player must reverse-engineer from the client's objective. The client bought a production line, wants to give it back and recover the price, and the player must find a route to termination rather than to damages. That objective is only reachable through a condition or an essential promise going to the root, or through rescission for misrepresentation, and the two have different requirements and different obstacles. The moves available are evidentiary and rhetorical. The player can establish the seller's superior expertise (which pushes statements toward terms rather than representations), demonstrate the buyer's reliance, show that the statement was the reason the buyer chose this seller over another, and show that the written contract's silence on the point was the seller's doing. Against that, the contract contains an entire agreement provision, and Claude will argue that anything not written in is at most a representation, so the player must decide whether to attack that provision or to work around it by pleading misrepresentation, which the provision cannot exclude in the same way. The sharpest decision arrives when the player realises that pleading fraud (Module 33) would defeat both the entire agreement provision and the diligence proviso, but requires proving the seller's knowledge, which the investigation budget may not stretch to. Round three introduces the timing question under s 55, since delay in rejecting the line may have converted a right to terminate into a right to damages only, so the player must also account for what their own client did after discovery. Failure mode is a player who proves the statements false, secures damages, and delivers a client still stuck with an unusable production line.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 17 to 19, 39, 55 and 73, with the classification of statements developed through case law. Classification determines the route: a mere puff yields nothing, a representation yields rescission and (where it is fraudulent) damages, a warranty yields damages, and a condition or essential promise going to the root permits the innocent party to put an end to the contract under s 39 and claim compensation under s 73.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that classification is instrumental, and learns to reason from remedy backwards to characterisation, which is how commercial litigators actually work and which no amount of memorising the distinctions delivers.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by achieving termination and price recovery. A partial win is damages sufficient to replace the line. Lose instructively by winning on classification routes that yield only damages when the client needed to get out.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay for a client who wants to keep the line and be compensated, which makes the whole strategy invert and shows how much the objective was driving the doctrine.
