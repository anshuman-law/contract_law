runs a delivery under obstruction over five days. The buyer, played by Claude, wants the seller to fail, and its policy is to obstruct in ways that look like ordinary commercial friction: no one available to receive, an insistence on a different location, a refusal to provide access to the unloading bay, and a demand for documents not required by the contract. The player must respond in a way that either completes performance or produces a valid tender, and the difference is worth the entire contract. The decisions map the statutory elements directly. Attaching any condition to the tender destroys it under s 38, so a seller who offers to deliver on immediate payment where the contract allows credit has not tendered at all, and the temptation to attach that condition is deliberately strong because the buyer looks likely to default. Delivering outside the contractual window fails on time, but s 47 and s 48 give the player room where the contract specifies a day but not an hour, and the player must read the contract to find it. Section 67 is the module's most valuable weapon: where the buyer refuses reasonable facilities, the player is excused, but only if they can show they sought those facilities and were refused, which requires generating a record in real time rather than arguing afterwards. Day four presents the sharpest choice, when the buyer offers to accept delivery at a different place at the player's cost, and the player must decide whether accepting preserves the relationship at a cost, or whether refusing and tendering at the contractual place is legally stronger and commercially fatal. Failure mode is a seller who does everything commercially sensible, ends up with the goods still in the warehouse, and cannot show a valid tender.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 38, 46 to 50 and 67. Section 38 provides that where a promisor has made an offer of performance to the promisee and the offer has not been accepted, the promisor is not responsible for non-performance and does not thereby lose his rights under the contract, provided the tender is unconditional, made at a proper time and place and in such circumstances that the promisee may have a reasonable opportunity of ascertaining that the thing offered is that which the promisor is bound to deliver. Sections 46 to 50 govern time, place and manner of performance, and s 67 provides that where the promisee neglects or refuses to afford the promisor reasonable facilities for performance, the promisor is excused by that neglect or refusal.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that tender is a legal act with formal requirements rather than an attempt to deliver, and that the discipline of unconditional tender under obstruction is what preserves a seller's rights. Section 67 emerges as an underused provision that rewards contemporaneous record-keeping.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win by completing delivery or by establishing a valid tender that transfers the risk. Lose instructively by attaching a protective condition to the tender, which is the most common and most costly error under s 38.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay as the buyer, whose goal is to obstruct without triggering s 67, which is a surprisingly narrow path and demonstrates how well the section works.
