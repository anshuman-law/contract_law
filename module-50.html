is a two-sided engagement. In the first half the player is in-house counsel drafting the terms for a subscription service, with a Business Value target that cannot be met by deleting everything risky. They must therefore find the versions of each term that retain most of the value at least risk: a liability cap at a defensible multiple rather than a total exclusion, termination rights that are reciprocal or tied to defined causes rather than unilateral and unreasoned, a deposit proportionate to actual risk rather than punitive, and assignment rights that do not prejudice the consumer. In the second half the same contract is attacked, and the player switches to consumer counsel. Now the three routes matter distinctly: CPA unfairness has a defined statutory test and a forum with power to declare terms void; s 23 public policy under the Brojo Nath line reaches unconscionable terms in standard form contracts even outside the consumer relationship but demands a higher showing; s 28 reaches terms that restrict enforcement of rights, including short claim windows, and is a different attack entirely because it does not require unfairness at all. Choosing the route determines which facts matter, and running the wrong one wastes the strongest evidence. Claude defends by disputing the consumer characterisation on one contract (a business subscriber), which closes the CPA route and forces the player onto s 23, where the standard is harder. Round three introduces a regulator considering a class-wide direction, so the player must think about which term is worst across the population rather than worst for their individual client. Failure mode is an attacker who runs the CPA route against a non-consumer and loses the case on characterisation before reaching the merits.

DOCTRINAL SPINE

ICA ss 23 and 28 with CPA ss 2(46), 49(2) and 59(2). CPA s 2(46) defines an unfair contract as one between a manufacturer or trader or service provider on one hand and a consumer on the other, having such terms as cause significant change in the rights of the consumer, and lists examples including excessive security deposits, disproportionate penalties, unilateral termination without reasonable cause, and assignment to the consumer's detriment. The State and National Commissions have power under ss 49(2) and 59(2) to declare such terms null and void. Central Inland Water Transport Corporation v Brojo Nath Ganguly supplies the s 23 route outside the consumer context.

STRATEGIC PAYOFF

The player learns that Indian law polices one-sided terms through several unconnected doors, each with its own key, and that identifying the door is the first and most consequential decision. This module also closes the loop opened in Module 2, where the player set the very rules they are now working inside.

WIN AND LOSS LOGIC

Win as drafter by hitting Business Value with aggregate exposure under threshold; win as attacker by striking at least three terms on correctly chosen routes. Lose instructively by drafting a total exclusion of liability for a fundamental obligation, or by attacking on a route the relationship does not support.

REPLAY HOOK

Replay with a business-to-business contract only, which removes the CPA door entirely and shows how much protection is consumer-specific.
